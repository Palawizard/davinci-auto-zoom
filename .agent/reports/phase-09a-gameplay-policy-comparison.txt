PHASE 9a — CANDIDATE GAMEPLAY POLICY vs DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP3, WINDOW BY WINDOW
=========================================================================

Date: 2026-08-18. Produced by `gameplay-study` (dry run: nothing was placed, no preview was
created, no gameplay asset was ever appended to any timeline).

    .venv/bin/python -m davinci_auto_zoom gameplay-study \
      --confirm-resolve-render-test \
      --project davinci-auto-zoom-test \
      --source-timeline DAZ_INPUT --reference-timeline DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP3 \
      --config config.example.toml

The measurement this comparison rests on is `.agent/reports/phase-09a-mvp3-gameplay-analysis.txt`.
Read that first: it is where the phase's actual result lives, and the result is that no
measured signal predicts the human's gameplay decisions.

**The two problems are reported separately throughout**, per D057:

    problem A   GAMEPLAY or X0?          -> the "manual / proposed" columns
    problem B   if GAMEPLAY, where?      -> the entry/exit columns

A rule can be right about A and 90 frames wrong about B, and this phase's rule is exactly
that. Collapsing the two into one accuracy number would hide the only useful thing here.


THE RULE BEING COMPARED
------------------------

    a would-be-X0 window becomes GAMEPLAY
      unless it is shorter than min_window_ms (500 ms)
      or it is the tail of the timeline

    entry anchor = start of the silence window, snapped to a hard cut within [-120, +120] ms
    exit  anchor = end   of the silence window, snapped to a hard cut within [-120, +120] ms

Zero fitted parameters. `use_secondary_audio` and `use_video` exist in
`GameplayPolicySettings` and are what the ablation switches; the candidate rule reaches its
score without consulting either (`gameplay_by_default = true`).

A "mixed" window is counted as agreement for problem A: the human did put gameplay in it, so
"this window should carry gameplay" is the right answer. *How much* of it they filled is
problem B, and that is where the mixed windows are scored.

**The whole error is one shape and it is worth naming.** Every divergence is a false
positive, and three of the four (10, 11, 12) are consecutive windows in the same stretch —
frames 218189 to 218599, the one part of the delivery where the creator talks in short bursts
and the editor stayed on the facecam ladder without ever cutting to the game. The remaining
one, gap 1, is the timeline's second window. Nothing measured in this phase distinguishes
that stretch: its secondary audio spans 8% to 81% activity and its motion spans 1.55x to
1.77x, both squarely inside the range of windows that *did* become gameplay.

That is a property of the section, not of any window in it, and it is the single most
promising thing to ask the creator about.

**The entry rule is not solved and this table is the evidence.** The systematic sign is the
whole story: 7 of 10 are negative, i.e. the proposal opens the game *earlier* than the editor
did, by up to 170 frames (2.8 s). The editor does not move to gameplay when the creator stops
talking — they wait, by 0 to 166 frames with no cluster (analysis report section 6), and
nothing measured here predicts how long.

The two positive outliers are both explained and neither is a taste difference:

  - gap 2, +77: the manual entry sits *inside* burst 1, and +77 is exactly the burst-extent
    overrun Phase 8c already recorded for that burst. Fixing Phase 8b would move this to ~0.
  - gap 5, +7: the manual entry is 7 frames inside burst 4, same cause, much smaller.

Cut snapping fired on 4 of 10 entries and helped none of them: in every case the cut it found
was 4-6 frames from the raw anchor while the manual entry was 53-170 frames away. It did no
harm either — which is the behaviour that makes a snap window safe to keep — but the facecam
`[-120, +120] ms` window is carried here on the strength of doing nothing, not on evidence
that it is right for gameplay (D060).

**The exit rule works, and it works because the exit anchor is the creator's voice.** The
silence window's end *is* the next burst start, so "exit where the window ends" and "exit
when he starts talking again" are the same rule, and the human agrees with it to within a
single frame in 6 of 10 cases.

This matches the manual measurement independently (analysis report section 7): the nine
`gameplay_to_face_x1` exits sit -1 to +41 frames from the next burst start, while the nearest
hard cut is 87 to 152 frames away in seven of nine. **The exits are placed on the voice, not
on the cut list.**

The one bad exit is gap 3 (+39), and it is caused by cut snapping, not by the anchor: the
raw anchor 216679 was 38 frames from the manual 216641, and the snap moved it one frame
*further* onto the cut at 216680. That is one case out of ten and inside the noise of a
single timeline, but it is the only measured instance of gameplay cut-snapping doing harm,
and it is an argument for a narrower exit window rather than a wider one.

The single `gameplay_to_x0` exit (episode 2, gap 3) is also the only exit the editor placed
*before* the next voice rather than on it — 38 frames early, exactly on a hard cut. One
instance is not a rule, but it is consistent with: you leave the game *for the face* when he
talks, and *for nothing* when the shot ends.

5. WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
-----------------------

- Nothing here is a fit. The rule has no parameter tuned against MVP3; its two exclusions are
  structural. That is also why its 11/15 should not be read as an accuracy figure that will
  transfer — it is one timeline, 15 windows, and 10 of the 15 labels are "gameplay".
- The `mixed` windows are counted as gameplay for problem A. Under a stricter reading (the
  window must be *mostly* gameplay) the rule would score worse, and the entry deltas in
  section 2 are precisely the reason: the human fills 9% to 70% of a mixed window, the
  proposal fills all of it.
- No claim is made about which of A2/A3 is the game and which is other people. The track
  names in this project are positional and Resolve renames them on deletion.
- The secondary-audio and video renders stay available as measurement, and the
  recommendation from the ablation is that **neither belongs in a runtime planner**.
