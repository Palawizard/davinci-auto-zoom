2. THE FIRST IMPORTANT FINDING: 14 BURSTS, 14 CYCLES
-----------------------------------------------------

The planner detects 14 editorial bursts on `DAZ_INPUT` (15 speech segments, 1 pause bridged).
`DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2` contains 14 manual zoom cycles, and they align 1:1 in order.

This closes a question carried since Phase 4. "x1 over-triggering — 14 planned vs 12 manual"
was recorded as a known defect against `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP`. **It was not a defect.** MVP2, the
newer manual edit of the same material, has 14 cycles in the same places. The old reference
was simply a looser edit. Nothing was tuned to achieve this; the burst logic is byte-identical
to Phase 6.

4. ANSWERS TO THE QUESTIONS THIS PHASE ASKED
--------------------------------------------

> After how much continuous speech does x1 become x2?

Between 43 and 88 frames after the zoom opens; median 58 (~970 ms at 60 fps). There is real
spread, but no outlier: the whole set fits in 45 frames.

> After how much more does x2 become x3?

Unanswerable from this material. **There is exactly one x3 in the entire timeline**, at +109
frames. One observation is not a distribution, and this is the single weakest number in the
phase. It is recorded as such rather than dressed up.

> Are there minimum remaining durations before promoting?

Yes, and this is the strongest signal in the data. Measured on cycle SPAN (zoom start to
reset), the two populations are cleanly separable:

    stayed at x1 : 23, 40, 40, 44, 48, 48, 74, 75      <- longest is 75
    promoted     : 87, 88, 104, 113, 138, 204          <- shortest is 87

**No overlap, and an 11-frame gap between them.** A rule of the form "promote at +T frames,
but only if at least R frames remain" fires exactly when `span >= T + R`, so any threshold in
(75, 87] reproduces all fourteen human decisions. That is what makes a simple rule defensible
here: it is not a curve fit, it is a gap in the data.

The shortest x2 the editor ever holds is 27 frames (cycle 9), which independently confirms
that a minimum-remaining term exists in their head at all.

> Are promotions driven by cuts or by speaking time?

**By speaking time.** Of the 7 manual promotion frames (6 x2 + 1 x3), exactly one — cycle 3's
`FACE_X2` at 216735 — lands on a hard cut, and 216735 is one of 20 cuts in a 3555-frame range,
so one hit is what chance predicts. Widening the tolerance to ±8 frames adds no further hits.

The contrast with resets is the point: 8 of 14 manual resets sit exactly on a hard cut. The
same measurement, on the same timeline, gives a strong signal for resets and nothing for
promotions. **Snapping stays where the evidence is (D047).**

> When does a long speech deliberately stay at x1?

Cycles 1 (74 frames) and 11 (75 frames) are the longest that stayed. Both are just under the
threshold, so they are consistent rather than anomalous. There is no case of a genuinely long
manual cycle held at x1.

> Are there x2-without-x3 cases on long bursts?

Yes, and this is the honest limit of the model. **Cycle 7 spans 204 frames — the longest cycle
in the timeline — and the editor kept it at x2**, while cycle 13, at 138 frames, went to x3.
No monotonic duration rule can produce both. Cycle 13 is the last cycle of the video; the most
plausible explanation is that x3 was saved for the closing beat, which is a narrative decision
and not something a duration heuristic can see.

> Do x2->x0 and x3->x0 resets follow the same logic as x1->x0?

Yes. All 14 manual resets are 42 frames long regardless of level, all are adjacent to the clip
before them, and the cut-snapping pattern does not change with level: of the 8 resets on a
hard cut, 4 come down from x1, 3 from x2 and 1 from x3. The reset rule is level-independent;
only the asset changes. This was the expected answer and it is confirmed.


5. THRESHOLDS CHOSEN, AND WHY
-----------------------------

    promote_to_face_x2_after_ms     = 1000   (60 frames at 60 fps)
    min_remaining_after_face_x2_ms  =  350   (21 frames)
    promote_to_face_x3_after_ms     = 1800   (108 frames)
    min_remaining_after_face_x3_ms  =  500   (30 frames)

x2 — well grounded.
  1000 ms sits close to the median observed offset (58 frames). The pair sums to 81 frames,
  which falls inside the empty (75, 87] gap with margin on both sides — 6 frames above the
  longest x1-only cycle, 6 below the shortest promoted one. Choosing the midpoint of a gap
  rather than an edge is deliberate: the edges are single observations.

x3 — thin, and flagged as such.
  1800 + 500 ms = 138 frames, exactly the span of the only manual x3. The offset 108 is within
  1 frame of the observed 109. This is calibration on n=1. It is implemented because the phase
  needs a working x3 rule and this is the only evidence there is, not because the number is
  trustworthy. It is one config line to change.

Deliberately NOT done: no ML, no threshold search, no per-burst fitting. Two of the four
numbers came from a visible gap in the data and two from a single observation, and the report
says which is which.


6. WHAT A SIMPLE RULE CANNOT CAPTURE
------------------------------------

Recorded here so the plan-comparison report's divergences are not a surprise:

1. **Cycle 7 (204 frames, kept at x2).** Genuine human nuance. Any duration rule that reaches
   x3 on the 138-frame cycle 13 also reaches it on cycle 7. Unfixable within this scope, and
   arguably not a defect — an automatic x3 there is defensible, just not what the human did.

2. **Cycles 1 and 12 are burst-extent disagreements, not promotion errors.** In cycle 1 the
   planner's reset lands 77 frames after the human's; in cycle 12 the planner's burst opens
   123 frames before the human's (it is the one burst built from two speech segments). Both
   make the automatic cycle far longer than the manual one, and a longer cycle promotes. The
   promotion rule is behaving correctly on the burst it was given; the burst is what differs.
   **Fixing burst extent would remove two of the three level divergences**, which makes it the
   highest-value Phase 9 candidate.

3. Manual reset instances are 42 frames (the asset's native length); DAZ places 15 (the
   animation length). Unchanged from Phase 5 and still correct per D025 — the extra 27 frames
   are hold-at-X0, which is visually identical to no clip at all.
