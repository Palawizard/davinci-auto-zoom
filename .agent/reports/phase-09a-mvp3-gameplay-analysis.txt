================================================================================

Date: 2026-08-18. DaVinci Resolve Studio 21.0.4.5, project `davinci-auto-zoom-test`.
Source `DAZ_INPUT` [216000, 219555) @ 60 fps. Reference `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP3`, V3, 42 items.

Nothing in this report was produced by writing to a reference timeline. The state
reconstruction and the episode table come from a read-only `snapshot`; the three renders
behind the audio and video numbers all ran on scratch duplicates and are audited in

**Read section 3 before section 8.** The prior hypothesis this phase was asked to test —

main result, and it is stated before any candidate rule.

is consistent with a readable idea — you leave the game *for the face* when he starts talking,
and *for nothing* when the shot ends.


8. WHAT A CANDIDATE RULE CAN HONESTLY BE
-----------------------------------------

Question A (gameplay or X0?) and question B (exactly where?) are separate, and this material
answers them very differently.

**Question A: no measured signal works.** Best single-threshold scores, over 15 windows,
where the majority baseline "always gameplay" is 10/15:

    silence duration       11/15      secondary audio, mean level   11/15
    secondary audio active 10/15      secondary audio onsets        11/15
    video mean motion      11/15      video active fraction         10/15
    video p90 motion       11/15      hard cuts in window           11/15

Every one of these is the baseline plus at most one window, and every one has fully nested
class ranges. Fitting a threshold to reach 11 here is fitting one lucky negative.

So the candidate rule shipped in `GameplayPolicySettings` uses **no signal at all**:

    a would-be-X0 window becomes GAMEPLAY
      unless it is shorter than 500 ms  (no room for the move to read as an intention)
      or it is the tail of the timeline (the outro; the reference edit leaves it at X0)

    entry anchor = the start of the silence window,   snapped to a hard cut within +-120 ms
    exit  anchor = the end of the silence window,     snapped to a hard cut within +-120 ms

It scores 11/15 — matching the best any measured feature reached — with **zero fitted
parameters**, and its two exclusions are structural rather than tuned. Its failure mode is
legible and worth naming: the four false positives are gaps 1, 10, 11 and 12, and gaps 10-12
are three *consecutive* windows in the one stretch of the delivery where the creator talks in
short bursts and the editor stayed on the facecam ladder throughout. Whatever separates that
section is a property of the section, not of any window in it, and nothing measured here sees
it.

The `long_silence_ms` backstop is deliberately set to 100 s — effectively off. On this
material the longest silence stayed X0, so a "very long silence implies gameplay" rung would
be actively wrong, and it is left in the settings as a disabled, documented knob rather than
deleted, because one timeline is not enough to rule the idea out for other material either.


9. ABLATION
------------

All four families run the same `decide_gameplay` with `gameplay_by_default` switched off, so
they measure what the signals explain on their own.

    family                  correct   false positives   false negatives
    A silence only            5/15    -                 0,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,13
    B silence+audio           8/15    1,11              0,6,8,9,13
    C silence+video           8/15    10,11,12          0,3,9,13
    D silence+audio+video     8/15    1,10,11,12        0,9,13

    candidate rule (no signal) 11/15  1,10,11,12        -

Family A with the backstop disabled proposes nothing, which is why it scores 5 — the five
true negatives. B, C and D all land *below* the majority baseline of 10, and all three land
at the same 8: adding the secondary audio, adding the video, and adding both are equally
unhelpful, they simply fail on different windows. **D is not selected**, and neither is B
nor C.

Note what D's error profile shows. It inherits every false positive of B *and* every false
positive of C (1, 10, 11, 12) while fixing only one of their false negatives, which is the
signature of two signals that are individually wrong in different places rather than two
signals that combine. There is nothing here to build on.

The recommendation that follows is blunt: **do not put either signal in the runtime.** The
secondary-audio render and the video render cost several minutes each and, on the only
reference that exists, buy negative accuracy. They stay in the codebase as a measurement tool
(`gameplay-study`), not as a planning input.

**Every gameplay asset animates in 15 frames**, exactly like the six facecam ones, despite

to planning, the same way `X1_TO_X0`'s 42 always was.

Both gameplay clip families are **hold** clips, not fixed-length ones: the shortest
`*_TO_GAMEPLAY` instance is 45 frames and the shortest `GAMEPLAY_TO_X1` is 23, both well past
15, and the editor varies them continuously with the length of the moment. So a
`*_to_gameplay` placement runs from the move to whatever comes next, and a `gameplay_to_*`
placement is the same shape — which is the `FACE_X*` rule (D014/D045), not the `X*_TO_X0` one.

`X3_TO_GAMEPLAY` has no manual instance anywhere in MVP3, so its 15 frames are inferred from
the other five rather than measured. **This is the one number in the config that should be
confirmed with the user** before Phase 9b places anything with it.

`config.example.toml` now carries all twelve roles. The six gameplay ones are optional
capabilities: `REQUIRED_ROLES` is unchanged, and
`tests/test_planner.py::test_configuring_gameplay_assets_does_not_change_the_facecam_plan`
proves a config carrying them produces placement-for-placement the same facecam plan.


11. WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY MOVE THIS FORWARD
------------------------------------------

In descending order of expected value, and none of it implemented here:

1. **A second reference timeline.** Every conclusion above rests on 15 windows from one
   delivery. The negative result is robust to that (nested ranges are not a small-sample
   artifact) but the candidate rule's 11/15 is not.
2. **Ask the creator about gaps 1, 10, 11 and 12.** Four windows, one question: why did the
   game not come up there? That answer is worth more than another feature.
3. **Burst extent (Phase 8b).** Three of ten manual entries sit inside a detected burst, and
   episode 1's -77 frames is exactly Phase 8c's recorded burst-1 overrun. Fixing burst extent
   would clean up the entry measurements before anyone tries to model them.
4. **Entry anchoring**, which is untouched: 7 of 10 entries are nowhere near a cut and range
   0-166 frames into their window. The exit rule is solved; the entry rule is not even
   sketched.
