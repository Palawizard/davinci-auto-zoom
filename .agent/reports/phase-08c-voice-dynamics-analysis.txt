PHASE 8c — VOICE DYNAMICS AGAINST DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2
=================================================

Run: 2026-08-18, DaVinci Resolve Studio 21.0.4.5, project `davinci-auto-zoom-test`.
Mode: one `plan-probe` (its own scratch render, created and deleted) plus a read-only
`snapshot --json`. Nothing was written to `DAZ_INPUT`, `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP`, `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2` or
any existing `DAZ_AUTO_PREVIEW_*`.

Question this phase had to answer BEFORE choosing any threshold: does the human editor's
choice of FACE_X2 / FACE_X3 line up with something audible inside the burst — a drop in the
voice followed by a pick-up — or does it not?

Answer: **it lines up, and tightly.** All seven manual promotions in `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2` sit
within 8 frames of a detected voice recovery, six of them within 3. That is the finding the
rest of the phase is built on.


0. THE SIGNAL, AND WHY IT IS SHAPED THIS WAY
--------------------------------------------

Envelope (`speech/energy.py`), built from the SAME normalized 16 kHz PCM the VAD already
consumes — one Resolve render, one ffmpeg pass, two readers (D050):

    window    30 ms      long enough to average a pitch period at any voice, short enough
                         that a syllable boundary survives
    hop       10 ms      0.6 frames at 60 fps; finer than the frame grid the plan lands on
    smoothing 30 ms      3-point moving average, EDGE-padded (zero padding invents a loud
                         burst at each end of the render, exactly where real bursts start)
    value     dBFS RMS, floored at -140 dB so digital silence has a defined value

Detection (`domain/dynamics.py`) is entirely relative:

    reference = 75th percentile of the envelope inside the CYCLE   (its own voice level)
    low line  = reference - promotion_min_drop_db
    valley    = maximal run below the low line
    recovery  = first point at/after it back above reference - promotion_recovery_within_db

Because every threshold is a dB *difference* against the cycle's own level, multiplying the
whole waveform by any constant shifts every point equally and cancels. Verified two ways:
`tests/test_dynamics.py` (+/-12 dB gives byte-identical decisions) and, during calibration,
by re-running the full 14-cycle plan at gains x0.25, x0.5, x2 and x4 — identical anchors in
all four cases, 14/14.


1. THE POSITIVE EVIDENCE: EVERY MANUAL PROMOTION IS ON A RECOVERY
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Nearest detected recovery to each of the seven manual promotion frames, with the valley that
produced it (drop and recovery in dB against the cycle's voice level):

  burst  manual        nearest recovery  delta   drop     recovery  valley
   0     FACE_X2 216088   216094          +6     31.7 dB  26.2 dB   140 ms
   3     FACE_X2 216735   216743          +8    118.1 dB 112.6 dB   220 ms
   7     FACE_X2 217628   217629          +1     41.9 dB  37.0 dB    70 ms
   8     FACE_X2 217958   217959          +1     44.3 dB  38.4 dB    60 ms
   9     FACE_X2 218156   218157          +1     23.1 dB  17.5 dB    20 ms

  13     FACE_X3 219098   219099          +1     38.8 dB  38.3 dB    80 ms

Median delta +1 frame, maximum +8. The two largest (bursts 0 and 3) are the two deepest
valleys, where the voice takes a few frames to climb back out of a full silence.

For contrast, the same seven frames against the hard cuts on V1:

  +44, 0, -27, +41, +27, +89, +29 frames

One of seven on a cut — chance, in a 3555-frame range with 20 cuts. This reproduces Phase 8's
D047 measurement exactly and is why cut snapping for a promotion is a small *correction* to an
audio anchor and never the anchor itself (section 5).


2. THE NEGATIVE EVIDENCE, WHICH IS THE HARD PART
-------------------------------------------------

Speech contains a dip roughly every second. The live run finds **63 valleys** inside the 14
cycles; the human made **7** promotions. So the honest question was never "can a valley
explain a promotion" but "can a rule reject the other 56".

Census of the 63, from the live plan-probe:

    signal rejected them
      valley_too_short (< 30 ms below the line)              13
      no_recovery_before_burst_end                            9
    the edit rejected them
      previous_animation_unfinished (inside the 15 frames)    7
      no_room_before_reset (< 400 ms of hold left)            3
      already_at_face_x3 (the ladder tops out)               17
    used
      -> face_x2                                              9
      -> face_x3                                              5

**No local feature separates the 7 human promotions from the other valleys.** This was tested
directly during calibration, on (low-span duration x depth) and on (pre/post level rise):

    manual promotions   (40 ms, 23.0 dB) ... (270 ms, 118 dB)
    valleys NOT used    (40 ms, 22.6 dB), (50 ms, 23.2 dB), (30 ms, 22.1 dB), ...

The populations overlap completely. Recorded here rather than papered over: a single-valley
classifier cannot reproduce this timeline, and any parameter set claiming to would be fitting
noise on n=7.

What DOES separate them is the *chain*: the ladder has two rungs, the first two usable cues
take them, and the animation/hold constraints remove the rest. The 17 `already_at_face_x3`
rejections are that mechanism doing most of the work.


3. CALIBRATION
--------------

Grid search over drop (12-24 dB), valley duration (20-200 ms), hold (250-500 ms) and three
envelope shapes, scored on peak level per cycle against `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2`. Chosen:

    promotion_min_drop_db        = 20
    promotion_recovery_within_db =  6
    promotion_min_valley_ms      = 30
    promotion_max_valley_ms      = 650
    promotion_min_hold_ms        = 400

Why each number:

  drop 20 dB — a PLATEAU, not a peak. 19, 20 and 21 dB score identically; so do (16 dB,
    50 ms) and (18 dB, 40 ms), which is the same boundary reached from a different direction.
    A parameter chosen from the middle of a flat region is the one that survives new material.

  recovery 6 dB — deliberately insensitive. Every value from 3 to 12 dB produces an identical
    plan, because a real pick-up overshoots the line by 15 dB or more. It exists to define
    "the dip is over", not to discriminate.

  valley 30 ms — below this a dip is a plosive or a glottal stop. 13 of the 63 valleys are
    rejected here, and none of them is near a manual promotion except burst 9's (20 ms), which
    is discussed in section 6.

  valley 650 ms — a GUARD, not a tuned value. A burst cannot contain a silence longer than
    `reset_after_silence_ms` by construction, so it never fires on this material (longest
    qualifying valley: 270 ms). It exists so that raising that gate cannot silently turn a
    genuine pause — which deserves a reset — into a promotion.

  hold 400 ms — the one genuinely editorial addition, and it is measured, not inherited.
    Phase 8's `min_remaining_after_face_x2_ms` gated on time since the BURST started; this
    gates on time after the PROMOTION. No manual promotion in `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2` is held for
    fewer than 27 frames (450 ms), so 400 ms = 24 frames sits just below every hold the editor
    actually made while still refusing a level that would flash for a few frames. Raising it to
    500 ms costs the real FACE_X3 in burst 13, which held for exactly 28 frames — the reason
    the number is 400 and not a rounder 500.

Not done, on purpose: no ML, no per-burst parameters, no automated search over anything the
runtime can see about `DAZ_OUTPUT_MVP2`. The runtime never reads that timeline.

**9 of 14 overall — and 9 of 9 on every cycle whose burst matches the human's, 0 of 5 on the
five where it does not.** The correlation is total. Every remaining disagreement about a LEVEL
is a disagreement about a burst EXTENT: a burst that opens 14-123 frames early, or closes 77
frames late, contains audio the human's cycle never covered, and that extra audio contains
extra breaths.

This is a different and more useful result than Phase 8's 11/14. The duration rule scored
higher while explaining nothing (it could not produce a 204-frame x2 and a 138-frame x3 at the
same time, D047); this rule scores lower and its every error has a single named cause that is
already the next planned phase (8b, burst extent).


5. CUT SNAPPING — MEASURED PER TRANSITION CLASS, NOT ASSUMED
-------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 6 measured the RESET window ([-120 ms, +350 ms]) on sentence ends. That measurement does
not transfer to a zoom-in anchor, so it was redone.

ENTRY (x0 -> face_x1). Manual x1 starts against the hard cuts on V1:

    exactly on a cut  : bursts 1, 3, 4, 11 (4 of 14)
    burst start there : -2, +1, 0, 0 frames from that cut
    nearest cut to any OTHER burst start: 61 frames (burst 10), then 62 (burst 6)

So a window of +/-120 ms (7 frames at 60 fps) catches all four real ones with an **8x margin**
to the first wrong candidate. Symmetric, unlike the reset: a zoom-in anchor has no systematic
bias, whereas a VAD end is a few frames late by construction (`speech_pad_ms`).

PROMOTION (face_x1 -> face_x2 -> face_x3). Same window, and the measurement says it will
almost never fire: only 1 of 7 manual promotions is on a cut, and of the 14 cues the live plan
used, exactly **1** found a cut within 7 frames (burst 7's face_x3, snapped -3 frames onto
217601). That is the intended behaviour: the cut is a small correction to an anchor the audio
already chose, never a reason to make a move.

Live totals for this run:

    entries     10 direct,  1 backward,  3 forward
    promotions  13 direct,  1 backward,  0 forward
    resets       6 direct,  8 backward,  0 forward   (frame-identical to Phases 6, 7 and 8)

Chain constraints are applied to candidates, not after them: a cut nearer the anchor that
would truncate the previous animation, overlap the previous cycle or leave the new level no
room is rejected in favour of the next valid candidate, and if none is valid the transition
stays exactly on its raw audio anchor.


6. HONEST LIMITS
----------------

1. **Burst 9's manual x2 is matched by a valley the signal rejects.** The human promoted at
   218156; there is a recovery at 218157 (+1) whose valley is only 20 ms long, below the 30 ms
   floor. The cycle still ends at x2, because an earlier qualifying cue at 218130 promoted it
   — right level, wrong frame by 26 frames. Lowering the floor to 20 ms would fix this one
   frame and break three other cycles.

2. **The frame agreement is weaker than the level agreement.** Where both the human and the
   planner promote, the deltas are -50 to +8 frames, not the +1 median of section 1 — because
   the planner takes the FIRST usable cue and the human sometimes takes a later one. Nothing in
   the audio distinguishes them. This is the honest residual ambiguity of the model.

3. **n = 14 cycles, 7 promotions, one timeline, one speaker.** Every number here is calibrated
   on a single edit. The parameters sit on plateaus rather than spikes, which is the best
   available evidence that they will transfer, but it is not the same as evidence that they do.

4. **The 63-valley census is a property of this delivery.** A faster or breathier speaker
   produces more valleys per second; the ladder's two rungs bound the damage, but the choice of
   WHICH two would change.

5. Phase 8's known divergence is now differently explained, not resolved: burst 7 (the longest
   cycle, kept at x2 by the human) reaches x3 here as well — but because its burst opens 41
   frames early and collects an extra breath, not because it is long.
