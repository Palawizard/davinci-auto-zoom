3. THE TWO QUESTIONS THIS COMPARISON EXISTED TO ANSWER
------------------------------------------------------

> Does the new logic explain why some long phrases must NOT go to x3?

Partly, and honestly: **not through length**. Burst 7 is the case Phase 8 flagged as
unexplainable — 204 manual frames deliberately kept at x2 while a 138-frame cycle went to x3.
The dynamics model also promotes it to x3, so the divergence survives. What changed is the
CAUSE, and the cause is now measurable: burst 7 opens 41 frames before the human's cycle, and
the extra 41 frames contain a qualifying recovery at 217525 that the human's cycle does not
contain at all. The same is true of bursts 6 (-14), 8 (-18), 12 (-123) and 1 (+77 at the end).

Fix the extent, and the level follows: **on the 9 cycles whose burst matches the human's, the
new model matches the human's peak level 9 times out of 9. On the 5 where it does not, 0 of
5.** No cycle disagrees for any other reason. Phase 8 had no such split — its 3 errors were 2
extent problems plus 1 genuine "no monotonic duration rule can do this".

> Does a shorter phrase reach x3 when it contains two real breaks?

Yes, and burst 13 is the clean demonstration. It spans 138 frames, the human took it to x3, and
the new model reaches x3 at 219099 — **one frame** from the human's 219098 — because the
delivery contains exactly two qualifying breaks (at 219041 and 219099). Phase 8 also reached x3
here, but only because 138 frames happened to equal the threshold it was calibrated on: it had
one observation and fitted it. The new model gets there from the audio, and would still get
there if the same delivery were 90 frames long.

The converse holds too: the longest flat stretch in the material — a burst with no qualifying
valley — now stays at x1 no matter how long it runs. The duration model could not express that
at all.
