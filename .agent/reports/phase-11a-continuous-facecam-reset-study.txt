PHASE 11a — CONTINUOUS-TALKING FACECAM RESET STUDY
Word-aligned French transcript + cut semantics on `bluescreen 2`
2026-08-19 · branch dev · RESEARCH, NOT SHIPPED

This report contains no sentence the creator spoke. Cuts are named by island, index and
absolute frame; the transcript, the rendered audio and the per-cut context stayed local,
gitignored and were deleted after the study (AGENTS.md, "User media").


0. WHAT THIS PHASE ASKED, AND THE ONE-LINE ANSWER
=================================================

The shipped facecam profile resets to X0 after a silence gate (`reset_after_silence_ms`,
650 ms between speech segments). In this second video type the creator talks almost
continuously and cuts most pauses out, so the question was whether a word-level French
transcript can explain the manual resets instead.

**Measured first, and it settles the motivation on its own:** Silero finds exactly TWO
speech segments in the labelled range — one per Short, each spanning its whole island. The
shipped rule would therefore produce at most 2 resets where the creator made 17. The silence
gate does not merely need retuning here; it has no events to fire on.

Answer: TRANSCRIPT HELPS BUT SEMANTICS REQUIRED. The full statement is in §12.

2. MANUAL STATE SEQUENCE — WHAT THE REFERENCE ACTUALLY DOES
===========================================================

The 57 placements were replayed through `domain/transitions.py` as a state machine.

    RESULT: zero state-machine problems. Every manual move is a legal transition of the
    frozen four-state graph. No overlap, no demotion, no jump from X0 to FACE_X2, and the
    state is correctly at X0 at the start of each island.

So the new video type needs **no new state and no new role**. The vocabulary is sufficient.

Cycle shape confirmed: every reset opens a new cycle `Xn -> X0 -> FACE_X1 -> (X2 -> X3)`.
There are 17 entries (`x0_to_face_x1`) and 17 resets.

--- THE FINDING THAT REFRAMES THE QUESTION -------------------------------------------

Asking "reset or nothing?" at a cut is the wrong question on this material. What each of
the 28 labelled hard cuts actually carries:

    face_x2_to_x0              7
    face_x3_to_x0              6
    face_x1_to_face_x2         4
    x0_to_face_x1              4
    face_x1_to_x0              2
    face_x2_to_face_x3         2
    (nothing)                  3
    ------------------------------
    TOTAL                     28

**25 of 28 hard cuts carry SOME transition.** Only three carry nothing (216322, 216812,
218553). The creator puts essentially every visual state change on a cut; what varies is
*which* change, and that depends on where the state machine already is.

Conversely, transitions are not all on cuts:

    entries on a cut          4 / 17
    promotions on a cut       6 / 23

So the reset/entry pair is cut-anchored far more strongly than promotions are. Promotions
float on the audio, exactly as the shipped planner already assumes — which is consistent
with the creator's statement that X1/X2/X3 behave the same way in this format.


3. RESET-TO-CUT DISTANCE, MEASURED NOT ASSUMED
==============================================

The distribution is bimodal and needs no tuning:

    15 of 17 resets sit at delta EXACTLY 0 from a hard cut
     2 of 17 sit at delta -54 and -64 frames (216758, 216991)

There is no intermediate case. The empirical linkage window is therefore **0 frames**, and
the old profile's ±120 ms cut-snap window was deliberately not reused: it would have been
seven frames of slack that this reference never uses.

The two off-cut resets are not noise, and they are the strongest evidence about re-entry:

    reset 216758 -> next FACE_X1 at 216823, hard cut at 216812  (entry is 11 frames after)
    reset 216991 -> next FACE_X1 at 217055, hard cut at 217055  (entry is ON the cut)

In both, the creator reset EARLY, in the middle of a clip, so that the FACE_X1 re-entry
could land on or beside the following cut. The reset is placed backwards from the entry, not
forwards from a trigger.


4. X0 DWELL — THE ~1 SECOND INTUITION IS NOT WHAT WAS EDITED
============================================================

Reset instances here are HELD: the clip starts at the editorial moment, animates for 15
frames, then sits at X0 until the next FACE_X1. So the placed length carries the dwell.

    measure             min  median    max      min ms   med ms   max ms
    anchor_gap           19    36.0     66         317      600     1100
    pure_x0_dwell         4    21.0     51          67      350      850

    (15 semantic resets; the 2 loop resets have no next FACE_X1 and are excluded)

**The creator's ~1 s intuition is not what the edit contains.** The median anchor gap is
600 ms and the median pure X0 dwell is 350 ms — a third of a second at normal framing. The
maximum anchor gap (1100 ms) is the only value near one second, and it is an outlier.

There is no plateau. The values are spread continuously from 19 to 66 frames, which is
itself the result: the dwell is not a constant the creator applies, it is whatever distance
falls out of placing the reset and then the entry independently. Combined with §3, the
consistent reading is:

    the entry is anchored (to a cut, or to where the voice restarts),
    the reset is placed far enough before it to read as a pause,
    and the gap between them is a consequence, not a setting.

A future implementation should therefore choose the re-entry anchor first and derive the
dwell, not hold X0 for a fixed duration. A fixed 1000 ms hold would be roughly three times
the measured median.

6. WORD TIMING QUALITY — THE FIRST GATE
=======================================

    whisperx words                        360
    faster-whisper native words           394
    aligned ratio                      1.0000
    partially aligned / unaligned      0.0000
    zero or negative durations              0
    negative inter-word gaps (overlaps)     0
    one frame at 60 fps                 16.67 ms

    word duration      p50  160 ms   p90  420 ms   max 2921 ms
    inter-word gap     p50   40 ms   p90   40 ms   max 3088 ms

Every token was placed. Nothing was invented: the pipeline classifies `aligned` /
`partially_aligned` / `unaligned` and never fabricates a timestamp, and on this audio the
unaligned bucket happened to be empty.

(A) WHISPERX vs FASTER-WHISPER NATIVE, 317 matched token pairs (difflib, `equal` blocks only)

    |start delta|   p50  147 ms   p90  273 ms   max 1363 ms    (p50  8.8 fr, p90 16.4 fr)
    |end   delta|   p50  125 ms   p90  284 ms   max 2605 ms    (p50  7.5 fr, p90 17.0 fr)

Read honestly: this is DISAGREEMENT, not error. Neither is ground truth. The two numbers
come from different mechanisms — wav2vec2 forced alignment versus Whisper cross-attention —
and the cross-attention timestamps are the known-weaker of the two. The disagreement is
large (a median of nearly 9 frames), so it rules out treating the two as interchangeable,
and it is not by itself evidence against WhisperX.

(B) ACOUSTIC PLAUSIBILITY — the independent check that decides the gate

    median dB inside a word     -31.11   (4932 frames)
    median dB between words     -39.93   ( 620 frames)
    separation                    8.82 dB

Frames the aligner calls "inside a word" are 8.8 dB louder than frames it calls "between
words". The boundaries track the actual envelope; they are not decorative.

(C) BOUNDARIES AGAINST THE PICTURE CUTS — the sharpest evidence

19 of the 28 hard cuts fall strictly inside an aligned word. That sounds alarming until it
is measured:

    frames of the word AFTER  the cut   min 1   p50  3.0   p90  5   max 16
    frames of the word BEFORE the cut   min 1   p50 15.0   p90 22   max 23

The word overhangs the cut by a MEDIAN OF 3 FRAMES (50 ms) and 5 frames at p90. So the cut
sits essentially at the end of the word — which is exactly what a creator trimming tight
does, leaving a few frames so the final consonant is not clipped. The apparent "cut inside a
word" is a 3-frame effect, not a word-sized misalignment.

GATE VERDICT: **word timing IS precise enough for this use.** Errors of 3-5 frames at edit
boundaries are well inside the tolerance an editorial decision needs at 60 fps. Word timing
is NOT the limiting factor, and `WORD TIMING NOT PRECISE ENOUGH` is not the conclusion.

Limitations, stated: no hand-labelled timing reference exists; the 3-frame overhang mixes
alignment error with the creator's own trim habit and cannot separate them; the comparison
is one speaker, one microphone, 92 seconds.

HUMAN LISTENING VALIDATION NOT PERFORMED. The agent environment has no audio playback and
the rendered voice was never listened to. Every timing statement above is instrumental.


7. ASR BEHAVIOUR AT EDIT BOUNDARIES
===================================

This material is exactly the hard case: pauses removed, takes spliced, sentences shortened,
28 jump cuts in 47 seconds.

    duplicated words at a splice        none observed
    obvious hallucination at a joint    none observed
    dropped first/last word at a cut    none observed
    two sentences merged into one       observed indirectly (see below)
    zero/negative durations             0
    overlapping words                   0

The transcript reads as continuous, grammatical French across every splice. The ASR does not
know a cut happened, and on this material that did no harm.

The one systematic weakness is punctuation, not words. Island 1 came back almost entirely
UNPUNCTUATED — a long run of enumerated steps arrived as one unbroken clause with no
sentence terminators at all, while island 0 was punctuated normally. That is a property of
the model's own prediction, and it is what makes family C collapse on island 1 (§10).

WhisperX also segments differently from faster-whisper (28 segments vs 40) on identical
audio, which is a further reminder that segment boundaries are model output, not structure.


8. LINGUISTIC STUDY
===================

DISCOURSE MARKERS (LEXICAL). The creator named `et donc` and `du coup` as seeds. They were
studied in position, never as a bare membership test: a marker counts only when it OPENS the
words after the cut or CLOSES the words before it. Categories used are transferable ones
(consequence, topic shift, enumeration, contrast, summary), not a list assembled after
reading the labels.

    cuts with a boundary-position marker        9 / 28
    of those, followed by a manual reset        5   (2 of them the loop resets)
    of those, NOT followed by a reset           4
    manual resets with no marker at all        10 / 13 (semantic resets)

    (family D scores TP=3, because the 2 loop resets are removed from its universe)

The seeds do occur at real boundaries — `du coup` opens the clause after cut 216224, which
is a reset — but they are neither necessary nor sufficient. Family D scores precision 0.429
and recall 0.231. **The user's intuition about `et donc` / `du coup` is directionally right
and quantitatively far too sparse to be a rule.** Ten of the thirteen semantic resets carry
no marker whatsoever.

Crucially, the same words appear mid-clause at cuts with no reset (216270 opens with a
continuation after `Du coup,`; 218683 with `et puis`), which is the exact failure the task
warned about: word presence is not boundary function.

ASR PUNCTUATION. Sentence-ending punctuation before the cut, or a capitalised word after it,
predicts a reset with precision 0.625 and recall 0.385 — the best precision of any signal
that DAZ could compute locally today. But it is confined to island 0: **all 8 cuts carrying
punctuation evidence are in island 0, and zero are in island 1**, because the model returned
island 1 essentially unpunctuated. Half the dataset is invisible to this feature.
Treated as a feature it is informative; treated as truth it is a model artefact.

SEMANTIC CONTINUITY (AGENT SEMANTIC — RESEARCH ONLY). Each of the 28 cuts was annotated by
the coding agent from the local transcript context (8 words each side, island-scoped) as
`SAME_THOUGHT_CONTINUES` / `DISCOURSE_BOUNDARY`, with a finer reason where nameable
(NEW_TOPIC, CONCLUSION_OR_CONSEQUENCE, NEW_STEP, TAKE_SPLICE, LOOP_END).

    DISCOURSE_BOUNDARY            18
    SAME_THOUGHT_CONTINUES        10

**This annotation was NOT blind.** The manual labels were visible when it was made. It is an
upper bound on what semantics could explain, not a measurement of an independent classifier,
and it is reported that way. No LLM API, no cloud call, no NLP model was added anywhere.


9. ACOUSTIC STUDY
=================

    speech segments in the labelled range        2 (one per island, each spanning it whole)
    hard cuts with any VAD pause                 0 / 28

Every labelled cut sits inside continuous detected speech. There is no pause to measure,
which is the creator's editing style showing up directly in the signal.

The envelope still has structure, so the acoustic family was built on a relative dB dip
instead, reusing the product's validated `promotion_min_drop_db = 20` unchanged rather than
tuning a new threshold to this dataset:

    dip depth at a cut     min 12.8 dB   median ~20 dB   max 37.3 dB
    energy discontinuity   -9.0 dB .. +10.8 dB across the cut

Neither separates the classes. Cuts with the DEEPEST dips are mostly NON-resets (218301 at
37.3 dB, 218553 at 35.9 dB, 216153 at 32.7 dB — all no-reset), while several resets sit at
13-15 dB (218235, 218735, 216224). The ranges are fully nested. Family B scores 0.385 /
0.385, essentially chance.

Adding acoustic support to the lexical/punctuation signals (family E) makes things WORSE,
not better: precision falls to 0.200 and recall to 0.077. The prosodic evidence and the
linguistic evidence disagree on this material.

The X2/X3 promotion engine was NOT retuned, examined for tuning, or touched.


10. ABLATION A-G  (+H, reported separately)
===========================================

Universe = the 28 labelled hard cuts. Families A-F and H are scored with the 2 loop resets
removed from BOTH sides (evaluation A of the task, semantics only); family G folds the
deterministic loop rule back in (evaluation B).

    family                                                 TP   FP   FN   TN   prec    rec     F1
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    A  cut only (STRUCTURAL)                               13   13    0    0  0.500  1.000  0.667
    B  cut + energy valley >= 20 dB (ACOUSTIC)              5    8    8    5  0.385  0.385  0.385
    C  cut + ASR sentence boundary (ASR PUNCTUATION)        5    3    8   10  0.625  0.385  0.476
    D  cut + discourse marker in position (LEXICAL)         3    4   10    9  0.429  0.231  0.300
    E  cut + lexical/punct + acoustic (LEX+PUNCT+ACOUSTIC)  1    4   12    9  0.200  0.077  0.111
    F  agent semantic continuity (AGENT SEMANTIC)          10    6    3    7  0.625  0.769  0.690
    G  F + deterministic loop override (F + USER RULE)     12    6    3    7  0.667  0.800  0.727
    H  F, only where a face state is held (F + STRUCTURAL) 10    2    3   11  0.833  0.769  0.800

H IS NOT ONE OF THE REQUESTED FAMILIES. It is a second reading of F, kept separate because
it changes the interpretation: a reset is only *legal* from a face state, so scoring F on
cuts where the picture was already at X0 asks it for a move the graph forbids. Gating F on
"a face state is currently held" — pure structure, no new signal — removes four of its six
false positives and lifts precision from 0.625 to 0.833.

Accuracy was deliberately not used to rank these. Family A has perfect recall and would win
any recall-only ranking while resetting on every second cut, which is the specific editorial
defect the task asked to avoid.


11. FAILURE ANALYSIS
====================

FAMILY A (every cut resets) — 13 false positives, 0 false negatives.
    Wrong at 216153, 216270, 216322, 216357, 216682, 216812, 217055, 218166, 218301,
    218460, 218504, 218553, 218683. Ten of these are pure diction jump cuts inside one
    clause (a take splice, a hesitation removed, an enumeration item continuing). Three are
    cuts where the state machine was at X0 already. A reset at any of them would land in the
    middle of a sentence — the defect this study exists to avoid.

FAMILY F/H — 6 false positives (2 after the state gate), 3 false negatives.

    False positives, and why the semantics were not wrong so much as incomplete:
      216682  a real new sentence, but the creator PROMOTED to FACE_X3 instead. The ladder
              was mid-climb and he chose to go tighter on the new idea rather than reset.
      216812  a real new sentence, but the picture was already at X0 (inside a held reset
              that began off-cut at 216758). Removed by the state gate.
      217055  a real new sentence with a contrast marker, but the state was X0 and this cut
              received the ENTRY. Removed by the state gate.
      218166  an enumeration opener, state X0, received the entry. Removed by the state gate.
      218301  a next-step marker, state X0, received the entry. Removed by the state gate.
      218553  a genuine boundary at face_x2 that the creator simply left alone — he was two
              cuts from the conclusion and let the shot run. NOT explained.

    False negatives, and these are the interesting ones:
      217373  a comma-level clause continuation: the words after the cut open a subordinate
              clause of the sentence the words before it began.
              Annotated SAME_THOUGHT_CONTINUES on linguistic grounds, and the creator reset
              anyway. He had been at face_x2 for ~120 frames; the reset reads as relief from
              a long hold rather than as a discourse decision.
      218235  an apposition naming the thing just introduced. Same pattern.
      218398  one item of a list of things added, one after another. The
              creator reset here but NOT at the next two items of the same enumeration
              (218460, 218504), which are linguistically identical. This one cannot be
              explained by the text at all — the difference is rhythm, not meaning.

    The 218398/218460/218504 triple is the clearest limit found in this study: three
    grammatically parallel enumeration items, one reset and two not.

FAMILY C — the 3 false positives (216682, 216812, 217055) are the same X0/promotion cases.
    Its 8 false negatives are almost all island 1, where the ASR emitted no punctuation.

FAMILY D — false positives at 216270 and 218683 are the textbook case: `Du coup,` and
    `et puis` present but functioning mid-clause, not as boundaries.


12. LOOP RULE
=============

    island 0   last hard cut 217835   manual loop reset at 217835   delta 0
               island end 217875      reset->island end 40 frames (667 ms)
    island 1   last hard cut 218828   manual loop reset at 218828   delta 0
               island end 218870      reset->island end 42 frames (700 ms)

**"The last hard cut of each content island gets a reset to X0" is EXACTLY what the
reference does, in both islands, with zero error.** The reset then runs to the island end
(40 and 42 frames), leaving the Short at X0 so it loops cleanly.

Recommended as a future DETERMINISTIC OVERRIDE, not as a learned class. Two islands is a
small sample, but the rule was stated by the creator in advance rather than discovered by
fitting, which is the strongest evidence a two-example rule can have.

17. KNOWN GAPS
==============

  - ONE reference, ONE creator, 47 labelled seconds, 28 cuts, 2 islands. Every number here
    is a description of this edit, not a calibration.
  - The semantic annotation was made by the coding agent WITH the labels visible. Family F
    is an upper bound, not a classifier score. A blind annotation would be worth more.
  - No human listening validation: the rendered voice was never heard.
  - The 3-frame word overhang at cuts mixes alignment error with the creator's trim habit
    and cannot separate them without a hand-labelled reference.
  - Island 1's missing ASR punctuation may be specific to this take; whether Whisper
    routinely drops punctuation on fast enumerations is unmeasured.
  - The state gate (H) was found by inspecting failures on this same 28-cut set. It is
    motivated by the transition graph rather than fitted, but it has not been validated
    against anything else.
  - `bluescreen 2` uses HELD reset instances (variable length carrying the dwell); the
    shipped executor places fixed-length resets. Diagnostic only, not scope.
  - Whether the X2/X3 promotion behaviour truly matches the shipped planner on this material
    was NOT measured. The creator asserted it, and this phase took the assertion as given.


18. RECOMMENDED PHASE 11b — THE SMALLEST NEXT EXPERIMENT
========================================================

NOT implemented here, and deliberately small.

    Label a SECOND continuous-talking Short from the same creator, and make one blind
    semantic annotation of its hard cuts BEFORE looking at the manual zooms.

Rationale: the only signal that reached a usable score is AGENT SEMANTIC, and its score is
currently unfalsifiable because the annotation saw the answers. One blind pass on unseen
material tells us whether F1 ~0.8 is real or an artefact — and that single number decides
everything downstream. If it holds, the question becomes "what local model can approximate
this?", which is a different and much larger phase. If it collapses, the transcript direction
is closed and no production work was wasted on it.

Two things should ride along, because they are free once the data exists:

    - re-check the loop rule on a third island (it is currently 2/2);
    - re-measure the X0 dwell, to see whether "the entry is anchored, the dwell falls out"
      reproduces or whether 350 ms median was a property of this take.

What should NOT be built yet: any profile abstraction, any embedding or LLM classifier, any
runtime NLP dependency, any reset policy in the planner. This phase produced data, and the
next one should too.


19. CONCLUSION
==============

Word-level French transcription works on this material and its timing is good enough: every
token aligned, the boundaries track the envelope by 8.8 dB, and at edit boundaries they land
within a median of 3 frames of the picture cut. Word timing is not the obstacle.

But the signals a local tool could compute from that transcript today — discourse markers in
boundary position, ASR sentence punctuation — top out at F1 0.476, and adding prosody makes
them worse. Only reading what the sentences MEAN gets to F1 0.800, and even that leaves three
grammatically parallel enumeration items where one is a reset and two are not.

Two things transfer immediately and cost nothing: the loop rule is exact (2/2), and gating on
"a face state is currently held" is worth +0.21 precision for free because it is just the
state graph.
